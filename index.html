How it works

Three steps, once. After that you just read a daily summary.

1

Resume first

Muse reviews your resume, suggests changes to make it interview-ready, and applies the ones you approve. Every application uses this version.

2

Trial run, immediately

Muse extracts everything it can from your resume, asks only what’s missing, then runs once right away — looking back over the last 1 week of postings.

3

Daily job, scheduled

Each day Muse searches the last 25 hours, applies to your best matches, and sends you a summary of what was applied, what’s stuck, and any resume tips.

How setup works (first time only)

Muse only asks about details it could not extract properly, or that are ambiguous.

  1. Resume first. You give Muse your resume. Muse reviews it and suggests changes to make it as strong as possible for landing interviews, and applies the ones you approve. Do not skip this: every application uses this resume.
  2. Muse extracts everything it can from the resume — name, contact details, experience, skills, education, certifications.
  3. Muse asks the remaining questions it needs:
    • Target job titles and any industries / companies to prefer or exclude
    • Location(s) and work type: remote / hybrid / on-site, and where each is acceptable
    • Employment type: full-time only, or are contracts OK?
    • Salary band: minimum–maximum and currency; what to do with postings that list no salary
    • Relocation: willing or not, and where
    • Work authorization: legally authorized to work in the target country? Sponsorship needed, now or in future?
    • Earliest start date / notice period
    • Screening answers: years of experience per key skill, and a salary-expectation answer for forms
    • Approval mode: apply automatically wherever every answer is on file, or prepare everything for your approval first
    • Where you want the daily summary (this chat, email, or another channel) — Muse only asks you to sign in to something after you confirm the channel that needs it
    • Run time and timezone (e.g. daily at 5:00 PM), and the stop condition (e.g. until you land a job)
  4. Trial run immediately. The trial run looks back over the last 1 week of postings. If any posting needs a sign-in, Muse asks you at that moment — and the same applies in future daily runs.
  5. Then Muse schedules the daily job. Setup is done; you only read the daily summary and clear anything stuck.

Where Muse looks for jobs

A living list — whenever Muse finds a new worthwhile source, it adds it, starts using it, and notes the addition in that day’s summary.

LinkedIn Jobs Indeed (your country’s site) BuiltIn Wellfound Company career pages General web search

What the daily job does

  1. Searches the sources above for postings published in the last 25 hours matching your titles, skills, location/type, and salary band. (25 hours, not 24, so nothing falls into the gap between runs.)
  2. Keeps a log of every posting seen, so nothing is ever applied to twice.
  3. Verifies each posting is real and still open.
  4. Applies to at most 10 postings per day, using your resume.
  5. Submits only when every required field and screening question can be answered truthfully from your confirmed answers on file. Never guesses — especially about work authorization, sponsorship, relocation, start date, or salary.
  6. Stuck postings never block the rest. If one application needs your action (a sign-in, a CAPTCHA, an answer Muse does not have), Muse leaves it pending, moves on in a separate browser window, and lists it in your summary with exactly what it needs. Any new answer you give is saved for next time.
  7. Resume improvement loop. After each run, Muse compares your resume against the postings it saw and suggests improvements in the summary.
  8. Sends the daily summary to your chosen channel: applied (with confirmations), stuck, and resume suggestions.
  9. Asks if you want anything changed. Every summary ends by asking whether you want to adjust titles, salary band, locations, sources, the daily cap, or approval mode.

Scope: Muse’s job is to apply for jobs daily. Everything after applying — replies, interviews, offers — is yours to handle. Muse does not ask for your email sign-in to track replies or interviews, unless you choose email as your summary channel — then it asks once, after you confirm.

Get the template

This is the exact text to give your Muse. Copy it, download it as a .md file, or expand it below to read the job instructions Muse fills in from your setup.

Daily_Job_Application_Job_Template.md 
First, run a one-time trial run covering the last 1 week of postings. Then schedule a daily job at [time, timezone] that:
1. Searches the sources listed above (keeping that list updated as new worthwhile sources appear) for postings published in the last 25 hours matching my titles, skills, employment type, work model/locations, and salary band [min–max, currency]; excludes [excluded companies/types].
2. Keeps a log of every posting seen so nothing is ever applied to twice.
3. Verifies each posting is real and still open on the employer’s site.
4. Applies to at most 10 postings per day with my resume, and only when every required field/screening question can be answered truthfully from my confirmed answers on file. Never guess — especially about work authorization, sponsorship, relocation, start date, or salary.
5. If an application gets stuck waiting for my action, leaves it pending, reports it in the summary, and continues with the other postings in a separate browser window — one stuck posting never blocks the rest.
6. After each run, checks my resume against the postings seen and suggests improvements that would help me land interviews.
7. Reports daily to [chosen channel]: what was applied (with confirmations), what is stuck and what it needs from me, and any resume suggestions — and ends by asking whether I want any of my requirements changed, applying what I confirm to the next runs.
8. Runs until I say I have landed a job.
Publishing this page on GitHub Pages
  1. Create a new GitHub repository (public).
  2. Add this index.html to the root of the repository — and, if you like, the .md template file alongside it.
  3. In the repo, go to Settings → Pages, set Source: Deploy from a branch, branch main, folder / (root), and Save.
  4. Wait a minute, then visit https://<your-username>.github.io/<repo-name>/ — that’s your live page.
